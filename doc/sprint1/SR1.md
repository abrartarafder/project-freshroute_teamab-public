## Practices to Continue
- Pair programming for integration tasks: helped us resolve integration problems more quickly, ... 
- Weekly standups with clear next-24h commitments: helped us maintain clear communication and expectations about task progress and discuss any issues we ran into. Regular meetings also helped us adjust responsibilities quickly when someone was overwhelmed. 
- Keeping Trello/backlog items mirrored in repository docs to avoid losing history even when trello tools changed or became unavailable.

## Practices to Start
- Create more branches for User Stories when needed: This should make tracking changes easier, improve collaboration and reduce merge conflicts. 
- Improve use of project management tools: We did not fully explore Trello's features early in the sprint, which led to premium features ending with our free premium subscriptions mid-sprint. To avoid this during the next sprint, we will review available features at the start of the sprint and make sure changes in the trello are immediately tracked in GitHub. 
- Better task distribution: We noticed some members accumulated more hours than others due to uneven task assignment. To remedy this, we will assign tasks in a more balanced way and split tasks into smaller subtasks that can be distributed across multiple members. This should ensure all members contribute evenly and stay within expected workload limits.
- Have mid-sprint checkpoints for integration-heavy tasks: This helps us avoid delaying crucial, error-prone tasks to the last day of the sprint and gives us more time to fix any issues that may appear. 

## Practices to Stop
- Leaving backend–frontend wiring until the very last days of the sprint. Although the integration succeeded, this created unnecessary time pressure and increased the risk of bugs. 
- Relying on premium-only tool features like Trello without local backups. Our Trello board temporarily relied on premium features, like labeling and point tracking. When our premium access ended, we had to quickly recreate our board with other features to meet documentation requirements.
  
## Team Collaboration Reflection
- Our team maintained strong communication througout the sprint. Regular standup meetings and informal discussions allowed us to quickly coordinate work and help each other with any issues.
- All team members contributed to development and discussions, which helped maintain steady progress toward sprint goals.
- Going forward, tasks will be distributed more equally to make sure everyone meets and does not exceed expected workload limits. 
- Maintaining open communication will remain a priority in future sprints.
  
## Best/Worst Experiences in Sprint 1
- **Best Experience in Sprint 1:** Pair programming sessions that allowed us to complete several UI pages quickly, like the landing page and organic/ethically raised pages. In-person standup meetings were also helpful in fostering collaboration amongst team members and keeping everyone aligned on sprint goals. 
- **Worst Experience in Sprint 1:** Losing Trello premium mid-sprint and having to quickly recreate points/labels. Also, backend integration lagged behind frontend development early in the sprint, which resulted in a heavier workload during the last few days of the sprint.
